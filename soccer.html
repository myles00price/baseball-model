REFERENCE NUMBERS — NOT PLAYS

A goals model, not a pick service. Each team carries an attack and a defence rating fitted on every Premier League result since 2015 with recent matches weighted more (half-life about eight months), using Understat expected goals blended 70/30 with actual goals. The two ratings give an expected scoreline; every number on the card — win / draw / win, double chance, over/under, both-to-score, likely scores — is read off that same score grid.

Walk-forward tested on 2280 matches, 2020–21 through 2025–26, refit before every match date: log-loss 0.979 against Pinnacle’s closing line at 0.963 (lower is better); on over/under 2.5, 0.677 vs 0.674. Calibration is honest — it says draw 23% and draws happen 24% of the time — but calibration is not an edge. A third pass that started from the market’s own Friday price and let the model nudge it did not beat the Friday price either. The market knows everything this model knows.

Total goals comes off the same score grid and is calibrated on the same six holdout seasons: over 1.5 / 2.5 / 3.5 predicted 79 / 56 / 34% vs actual 79 / 56 / 33%. On over/under 2.5 it sits within four thousandths of the closing line — close enough to describe the market, not close enough to beat it.

Shots on target is a separate model, same architecture: each club carries a shooting and a shot-suppression rating, fitted on every match since 2015. Walk-forward over 4560 team-matches it predicted an average of 4.44 against an actual 4.47, its 80% ranges covered 86% (ranges on whole numbers run a touch wide by construction), and each PIT decile between 0.090 and 0.113 where a perfectly calibrated model shows 0.100. No public archive of shots lines exists to test against, so unlike the goals numbers this one is calibration-only.

Player shots on target allocates each club's validated team number to its shooters: a player's on-target rate comes from his own shot history (every shot he has taken, on target = goal or forced save), shrunk toward the league rate, decayed for recency, and adjusted for the opponent's shot suppression. Walk-forward over 7355 player-matches (2024-25 → now, given actual minutes): projected 38% to land 1+ and it happened 38% of the time; average projection 0.55 on target against 0.52 actual. It runs a hair hot on 2-plus (12.4% projected, 10.7% happened) — read the 2+ column with that in mind. The card projects per start — lineups are not known when it posts, and that extra uncertainty is not in those numbers. No public archive of player shots lines exists, so this too is calibration, not a beaten market.

The Ledger at the bottom grades every published number after each matchweek — win/draw/win against the closing line, the over/under calls, the ranges, every player projection, and a flat $100 paper record on anything the model liked by 3%+. Grades are final and misses publish too, same as the MLB board.

BEST US = the best American-odds price across DraftKings, FanDuel, BetMGM, Caesars at the last snapshot. DNB = draw-no-bet, the two-way moneyline, priced off the same grid. Lines move; these are for context. Nothing here is a play, nothing is staked, nothing is texted.
