This page exists because the model was tested honestly and lost. Before a single number went up it was run walk-forward across three past seasons — ratings rebuilt every week from games played before the date being predicted, never after — and graded against 11,508 real closing spreads. 2026-09-15NCAAM GOES UP AS NUMBERS ONLY - THE MARKET BEAT IT, AND HERE IS THE PROOF
College basketball joins the board tonight, and it joins it losing. The honest summary first: across 11,508 games with a real closing spread, our number missed the final margin by 9.65 points on average and the closing line missed by 8.71. The market was better by about a point, and betting our disagreement with it won 50.1% of the time - which loses money once you pay the vig. So this is a reference page. Numbers go up, plays do not, and nothing from this model will ever reach the text list. Some history, because it matters. This model used to live in a spreadsheet, and that spreadsheet claimed a 57% record - and, stranger, 78% on away teams against 43% on home teams. That 35-point gap is not something a basketball model can do; it was a bookkeeping artefact. The old tracker wrote a row for BOTH sides of every game, more than half the rows were never graded at all, and nothing anywhere recorded which side had actually been bet. So the 57% was really measuring which rows someone happened to type a result into. Re-graded properly from final scores - one row per bet, graded mechanically - it was 54.7%, on 446 games, which is not far enough above break-even to call anything. That is why the whole thing was rebuilt and retested from scratch rather than simply moved across. The rebuild was tested the way everything on this site is tested: walk-forward, meaning the team ratings used to predict any given game are built only from games played BEFORE it, never after. That distinction is the whole ballgame. Using end-of-season ratings to 'predict' that same season is the single easiest way to produce a model that looks brilliant and is worthless, and it is the trap most published basketball systems fall into. We also tried fifteen different settings of the model's two dials to make sure the answer was not just a bad choice of knob. None of them beat break-even. Not one. The most interesting number on the page is the one that looks least dramatic. At its sharpest setting the model gets within a third of a point of the closing line's accuracy - genuinely close - and still cannot beat it. That is what it looks like when a market has already priced in everything that is publicly knowable. The remaining gap is not a flaw we can engineer away; it is the part of a basketball game that nobody predicts. Worth seeing from the inside, which is why the page exists at all. What is still open: totals have not been tested here yet, and neither has the opening line, which in college football is the one place this site has ever found a real signal. Those get done before the season tips in November. If either clears the bar, it will say so here first - and if it does not, this page will keep saying no edge in large friendly letters.
