# Week 3 availability review — 2026-09-23

Yesterday's review left players unresolved: each carried a designation on the Week 2 official injury report, with
no current report to replace it. This is the follow-up, with today's evidence, and it decides who may be picked.

## The Week 3 official report exists, and it settles nobody

It carries 22 rows, all for Atlanta and Green Bay, who play on Thursday. Every row is practice participation.
Not one carries a game status. Thirty of the thirty-two teams have filed nothing for Week 3 yet, because their
games are on Sunday and those reports are filed later in the week — after our Thursday lock.

So no player on this board has a current official game status. That is not a reason to treat anyone as healthy.

## Who is held out of picks

Forty-one of the 364 projected players are on the do-not-pick list. The lock program refuses to pick a listed
player in a preview, a lock or a text. A player is listed if any of these is true:

| grounds | meaning | players |
|---|---|---|
| A | carried a game status on the last report that had them (Week 2), none since | 22 |
| B | appears on this week's report, practice participation only, no game status | 4 |
| C | today's injury feed designates him questionable, doubtful or out | 32 |

The letters overlap; the list is their union. Each entry records which applied and quotes the evidence.

Yesterday's list held three quarterbacks. An audit found that rule too narrow: it covered only players the feed
still called active after an old designation. It missed **Brian Robinson**, who is on this week's practice report
with an ankle, plays on Thursday, and was stopped by nothing else at all. He and **Jayden Reed** are on the list
now, and the rule was widened so the gap cannot recur.

Past the model's own gates, 243 of the 364 projected players remain pickable, against 249 without the list. It
uniquely removes six: Tua Tagovailoa, Joe Burrow, Chris Olave, Ladd McConkey, Brian Robinson and Michael Penix Jr.
The other thirty-five were already held out by the availability gate. Every team is still represented, and
Thursday's game alone keeps fourteen players. A Week 3 lock would still have plenty in it.

## The loosest part of the list, stated plainly

Six entries rest only on a Week 2 designation. Four of those players then played in Week 2, one is reported to
have cleared concussion protocol and to be starting on Sunday, and one has been named Thursday's starter. None of
that is an official game status, so under the standing rule they stay out. If the rule should instead treat
playing in the following game as settling the question, these six are the entries to revisit — that is a decision
about the rule, not about the evidence.
