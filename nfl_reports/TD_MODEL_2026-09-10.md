# ANYTIME TOUCHDOWN MODEL — DESIGN AND FIRST RESULTS (2026-09-10)

Built to Myles's four-step spec. Top-down and market-anchored: the game's spread and
total set the scoring environment, and the props engine's opportunity projections
carve it into player shares. Nothing here is a pick; the board shows it as PAPER.

## The chain

1. **Implied team points.** `total/2 ± spread/2` from the schedule's line. Checking
   these against posted team totals is on the weekly checklist; not yet done.
2. **Team touchdowns.** Offensive TDs = −0.647 + 0.1354 × implied points, fit on
   2016-2025 team-games (walk-forward: only seasons before the target). Plus a flat
   league-rate D/ST slice (0.13 per team-game). This is the user's Methods 2 and 3
   folded into one fit: TD-points share is *not* a constant — it climbs from 57% of
   points at a 16-point implied total to 70% at 30 — so a fixed percentage divided
   by six would be biased at both ends. The regression carries that curve.
3. **Pass / rush / D-ST split.** League split from the last three seasons
   (2019-24 reproduced exactly from play-by-play: 58.56 / 35.86 / 5.58, which
   validates the TD attribution). Hand overrides for coaching and personnel
   judgment live in `td_split_overrides.json`.
4. **Player shares.** Each player's projected carries and targets (from the props
   engine, so injuries, snap allocation and OUT redistribution flow through) are
   weighted by a TD-per-touch rate: usage-implied (`0.0021 + 0.352 × share of
   carries inside the 10`; `pos + 0.252 × share of targets inside the 20`) shrunk
   toward the position prior, then blended with the player's own observed rate
   (150 carries / 120 targets of prior weight). Shares renormalise to the team's
   projected pass and rush TDs. P(anytime) = 1 − e^−λ, times the chance he plays.

## What the data said (and what got cut)

- **Team finishing tendency does not carry over.** Once offensive TDs are isolated,
  a team's residual over the implied-points line has a year-to-year correlation
  of 0.02. The 0.35 seen on a first pass was a league-wide season effect. The
  market already prices finishing; the term stays in the code with a fitted
  weight of 0.016 — effectively off.
- **Opponent defence: 0.08 weight.** Not zero but small, and Myles's rule stands:
  early-season defensive numbers get little trust.
- **Team-specific pass/rush split is a wash.** Weighting a team's prior-season
  split at 0.32 and updating it in-season scored identically to the plain league
  split in the 2025 walk-forward (Brier .14716 vs .14718). Off by default; the
  override lever remains for personnel calls a person is confident in.
- **No calibration layer needed.** Platt scaling on the backtest made Brier worse
  (.1474 vs .1472). Raw probabilities ship.

## 2025 walk-forward backtest (weeks 2-18, 4,962 player-games, 20.5% scored)

| model | Brier | log loss |
|---|---|---|
| this model | 0.1472 | 0.468 |
| bottom-up engine (no market anchor) | 0.1496 | 0.474 |
| prior TD-per-game rate | 0.1573 | 0.596 |
| constant | 0.1632 | 0.508 |

Calibration by bin tracks within 1-4 points up to 60%; the 60-70% bin (41 games)
ran 9 points hot and the priced tier (P ≥ 40%, 499 games) ran 3 points hot
(48.6% projected, 45.7% actual). Treat the very top of the board as slightly
generous until a second season says otherwise. Team layer: offensive TDs MAE
1.048 vs 1.167 for a constant; projected 2.39 per team-game vs 2.45 actual.

## What is NOT proven

- Never tested against posted anytime prices. The books' Yes prices carry a
  hold of roughly 5-8 points; the EDGE column subtracts the best book's implied
  probability, so a positive number clears the vig on paper only.
- A price pilot (historical anytime odds, 10 credits per market-event) is the
  next honest step before any of this is texted.
- Return touchdowns are not assigned to returners; defensive scores are a
  league-rate slice, not player-level.
