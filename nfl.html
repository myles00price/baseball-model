THE ENGINE

Usage first: how many plays a team runs and who gets the targets and carries, blended from 2025 per-game rates and re-mapped to 2026 rosters. Efficiency is shrunk toward position averages, adjusted for the opposing defense at half weight, and every player's numbers are forced to add up to their team's totals. Projections are then recalibrated against a full season of walk-forward testing. Click any game for the Gameday view: season stats and how often each player beat the current projection last year.

  • Volume is allocated by PROJECTED SNAP SHARE (own history blended with the league norm for the player's depth-chart slot, chart current to Sept 8) times his on-field target/rush rate. 53-man active rosters only; IR and practice squad are off the board (217 players dropped in re-mapping).
  • 23 depth-chart rookies are projected for the first time — snap share from their depth slot, efficiency at position average until they play. Marked R; widest error bars on the board.
  • Week 1 runs on 2025 data — expect the widest error bars of the year.
  • Passing-yard props are permanently out: four separate tests said the market knows better.

MODEL UPDATES

2026-09-08INJURY ODDS ON EVERY PROJECTION - AND MYLES'S DEPTH-CHART READS

The board now prices injuries the way the games actually happen: probabilistically. Every skill player with an injury designation carries a judged chance of missing the game - 25, 50, 75 or 100 percent - drawn from the live injury wire and adjusted by what the reports actually say (a full practice reads very differently from a walking boot). Each team is then projected across every combination of who plays and who sits, weighted by those odds, and the board shows the blended result: when a number-two receiver is a coin flip to play, most of his targets flow to the number one in the half of the world where he sits, the rest cascade down the depth chart, and the number one's projection rises to match. A flagged player's own number is his line if he plays, since his prop is void if he doesn't. Fifty-eight players start the week as coin flips or worse; their cards say so in red, and no paper pick is ever made ON a player with real miss risk - only on the teammates whose numbers already carry the math. Two more things landed with it. Players stepping into new roles - promoted on the depth chart since last season - now have their old-role history half-weighted, so a receiver bumped to number two reads like one. And a hand-override file lets Myles's read of a depth chart beat the morning snapshot, which is how Matthew Golden went on the board as Green Bay's WR2 tonight. Two of the automatic judgments were caught and corrected before publishing: one quarterback flagged off a teammate's injury note, one receiver flagged because tests had ruled a concussion OUT - good news the machine read as bad.

2026-09-08PASSING PROPS RETURN - VOLUME ONLY, AND THE GATE CAUGHT A FAKE

Passing markets are back on the board, on new evidence and with one hard lesson attached. Passing attempts and completions - how many throws, how many connect - are volume questions, the thing this engine does best, and on seventeen unseen weeks of last season it beat the simple baseline on both. They join the pick pool with thresholds set at the same depth as the proven markets. Passing YARDS stays display-only: it failed last season's real-line test four separate ways, and no amount of beating a practice baseline erases a losing record against actual books. One catch worth confessing because it nearly went out the door: the first draft projected every quarterback in the league at about 23 attempts - a calibration layer stretching numbers against a stale yardstick - and five fake unders qualified as picks before the preview step caught the uniformity. The fix: passing volume displays as the engine's raw numbers, no stretching. Tonight those raw numbers sit just under the books everywhere - close enough to respect, not far enough to bet, which is exactly what an honest no-pick looks like.

2026-09-08MYLES CALLED THE LINES OUT - HE WAS RIGHT

The board's numbers looked wrong to the one person who reads them hardest, and the audit proved him right: star players were projecting 30 to 40 percent below what any sportsbook would post. Two causes, both fixed the same night. First, the engine hedges - every layer of caution it applies (pulling efficiency toward averages, blending in last season, tempering hot streaks) is individually correct, but stacked together they squeezed the top of the league into the middle. The elite projection was literally rarer in our numbers than elite games are in real life. The fix maps every projection onto the scale of what players of that rank actually average, so the number on the card now reads like a fair line - a top receiver shows 90 receiving yards, not 48 - while everyone keeps their order. An early draft of that fix had its own bug worth confessing: it briefly assigned one star negative receiving yards, because it was matching against single games instead of season averages, and every great player has one disaster game. Second, projections were splitting volume among more backups than ever share a real field at once, quietly taxing the stars; tightening that also measured out as slightly more accurate on four unseen months of last season. One honest note stands: a few big names still project below their reputation because their actual recent usage says so - the board trusts data over jersey sales, and the paper season will tell us which one of us is right.

2026-09-08WEEK 1 RE-RUN: FINAL ROSTERS, SNAP-SHARE ALLOCATION, ROOKIES ON THE BOARD

Every projection re-run the day before kickoff with the September 8 reality: final 53-man rosters only (cuts, injured reserve and practice squads are off the board), starting quarterbacks taken from each team's actual depth chart rather than career stats - which matters when a returning veteran has more career throws than the announced starter - and a smarter answer to the question of who gets the ball. Volume is now allocated the way the engine was designed to think: how much of the game is this player actually on the field for, times what he does when he is out there. A player's expected snap share blends his own history with what his current depth-chart slot typically earns, so a promoted player rises and a demoted one falls before a single 2026 snap is played. That change also opens the door the old method could not: 23 depth-chart rookies are projected for the first time, marked with an R and carrying the widest error bars on the board - their playing time comes from their depth slot, their efficiency from position averages until they show their own. Workhorse backs came up noticeably: splitting usage by snaps instead of raw per-game averages stops punishing stars for the games they left early.

2026-08-06THE OPENING-LINE TEST: THE MARKET DRIFTS TOWARD OUR NUMBER

A subscriber-grade question from Myles: if the picks break even against the final line, they should be ahead of the early line - so test it. The same four weeks of prop lines were pulled again, this time as they stood three days before kickoff, and the same picks were graded against both. He was right about the direction: identical picks score about a point better against the early line than the late one. The finding that matters more, though, is which way the lines MOVED. When a receptions line changed between Tuesday and kickoff, it moved toward our projection 21 times out of 27 - and rushing lines followed us 58% of the time. In this business that is the tell: the market spending all week correcting toward your number means your number was ahead of it. Passing yards showed the mirror image - the market moved away from us, its final verdict on that market, which is now dropped for good. The plan is unchanged in discipline and sharpened in focus: a full paper season on receptions, receiving yards and rushing yards, with picks made early in the week when the value exists, every one logged and graded, no money until half a season proves it.
